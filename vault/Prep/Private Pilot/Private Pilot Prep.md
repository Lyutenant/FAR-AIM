### pre-solo

- [[4-1-20|AIM 4-1-20 — Transponder and ADS-B Out Operation]] — Squawk 1200 for VFR with altitude reporting on; the transponder and ADS-B Out stay on from pushback to parking, ident only when asked, never squawk 7777, and avoid passing through 7500, 7600 or 7700 when changing codes.
- [[4-3-3|AIM 4-3-3 — Traffic Patterns]] — The standard traffic pattern: enter in level flight abeam midfield at pattern altitude (1,000 ft AGL for propeller aircraft unless the Chart Supplement says otherwise), all turns left unless RP is charted, turn crosswind beyond the departure end within 300 ft of pattern altitude, complete the turn to final at least 1/4 mile out, depart straight out or with a 45° turn beyond the departure end.
- [[61.3|§ 61.3 — Requirement for certificates, ratings, privileges, and authorizations]] — What you must have on you to fly: your pilot certificate, a government photo ID and, unless BasicMed or a listed exception applies, your medical — in physical possession or readily accessible in the aircraft, and shown on request to the FAA, NTSB, TSA or any law-enforcement officer.
- [[61.23|§ 61.23 — Medical certificates: Requirement and duration]] — A private pilot needs at least a third-class medical (or BasicMed); the class you must hold depends on the privileges you exercise, and how long it lasts depends on the class and your age on the exam date (the table in paragraph (d)).
- [[91.3|§ 91.3 — Responsibility and authority of the pilot in command]] — The pilot in command is directly responsible for, and the final authority as to, the operation of the aircraft; in an in-flight emergency requiring immediate action the PIC may deviate from any Part 91 rule to the extent the emergency requires, and must send a written report only if the Administrator asks for one.
- [[91.17|§ 91.17 — Alcohol or drugs]] — No crewmember within 8 hours of drinking, while under the influence, with an alcohol concentration of 0.04 or greater, or while using any drug that affects safety; and, except in an emergency, no carrying a passenger who appears intoxicated.
- [[91.107|§ 91.107 — Use of safety belts, shoulder harnesses, and child restraint systems]] — The PIC must brief everyone on how to fasten and unfasten their safety belt and shoulder harness, notify them to fasten before surface movement, takeoff and landing, and everyone must occupy an approved seat with the belt fastened at those times; a child under two may be held.
- [[91.113|§ 91.113 — Right-of-way rules: Except water operations]] — See and avoid always applies. Converging: the aircraft on the right has the right of way, but balloons, gliders, airships and aircraft towing or refueling rank above powered aircraft; head-on, both alter course right; the overtaken aircraft has the right of way; aircraft landing or on final have it over others, and the lower of two approaching aircraft — but neither may abuse it.
- [[91.205|§ 91.205 — Powered civil aircraft with standard U.S. airworthiness certificates: Instrument and equipment requirements]] — The instruments and equipment that must be installed and operable: paragraph (b) for day VFR (airspeed, altimeter, compass, tachometer, oil pressure and temperature gauges, fuel gauges, gear indicator, belts and harnesses, ELT and more), paragraph (c) adds for night VFR position lights, an anticollision light system, an adequate electrical source, spare fuses and a landing light if for hire; paragraph (d) is the IFR list. Anything inoperative goes through § 91.213.
- [[91.209|§ 91.209 — Aircraft lights]] — From sunset to sunrise an aircraft must show lighted position lights, and an aircraft equipped with anticollision lights must have them on at all times unless the PIC decides safety requires them off.

### solo-xc

- [[3-2-3|AIM 3-2-3 — Class B Airspace]] — Class B surrounds the busiest airports, typically from the surface to 10,000 ft MSL in tailored layers; an ATC clearance is required to enter, VFR aircraft receive separation inside, cloud clearance is simply clear of clouds, and the 30 NM Mode C veil surrounds the primary airport.
- [[3-2-4|AIM 3-2-4 — Class C Airspace]] — Class C is usually a 5 NM core from the surface and a 10 NM shelf from about 1,200 ft, both up to 4,000 ft above the airport, with a 20 NM outer area where services continue but participation is voluntary; two-way communications (your call sign used) before entry, transponder and ADS-B Out required.
- [[3-2-5|AIM 3-2-5 — Class D Airspace]] — Class D surrounds towered airports, usually from the surface to 2,500 ft above field elevation; two-way communications before entry (your call sign used), no separation services for VFR, and when a part-time tower closes the area reverts to Class E or Class G as the Chart Supplement states.
- [[3-2-6|AIM 3-2-6 — Class E Airspace]] — Class E is controlled airspace with no communication or clearance requirement for VFR: transition areas begin at 700 ft AGL (magenta vignette) or 1,200 ft AGL (blue vignette) around airports with instrument procedures, Class E otherwise starts at 14,500 ft MSL, and Federal airways are Class E from 1,200 ft AGL up to 18,000 ft.
- [[91.103|§ 91.103 — Preflight action]] — Before every flight the PIC must become familiar with all available information: for flights away from the airport or under IFR, weather reports and forecasts, fuel requirements, alternatives and any known ATC delays; for every flight, runway lengths and takeoff and landing distance data.
- [[91.117|§ 91.117 — Aircraft speed]] — Speed limits: 250 knots indicated below 10,000 ft MSL; 200 knots at or below 2,500 ft AGL within 4 NM of the primary airport of a Class C or D area; 200 knots beneath a Class B area or in a VFR corridor through it.
- [[91.119|§ 91.119 — Minimum safe altitudes: General]] — Minimum safe altitudes: always high enough to make an emergency landing without undue hazard if the engine fails; over congested areas 1,000 ft above the highest obstacle within a 2,000 ft radius; elsewhere 500 ft above the surface, or over open water and sparsely populated areas no closer than 500 ft to any person, vessel, vehicle or structure.
- [[91.129|§ 91.129 — Operations in Class D airspace]] — Class D: establish two-way radio communications with the tower before entering and maintain them inside; a clearance is required to taxi, take off or land at a towered airport; if the radio fails in flight under VFR you may land with basic VFR weather, visual contact with the tower and a light-signal clearance.
- [[91.130|§ 91.130 — Operations in Class C airspace]] — Class C adds to the Class D rules: two-way radio communications must be established with the facility providing ATC services before entry and maintained inside, and the aircraft must have a Mode C transponder and ADS-B Out.
- [[91.131|§ 91.131 — Operations in Class B airspace]] — Class B: an ATC clearance is required before entry; the PIC must hold at least a private pilot certificate (or be a student, recreational or sport pilot with the required training and endorsement, except at the airports listed in appendix D section 4); the aircraft needs a two-way radio, a Mode C transponder and ADS-B Out.
- [[91.151|§ 91.151 — Fuel requirements for flight in VFR conditions]] — Day VFR: enough fuel to fly to the first point of intended landing and then for at least 30 minutes at normal cruise; at night, at least 45 minutes.
- [[91.155|§ 91.155 — Basic VFR weather minimums]] — Basic VFR weather minimums by airspace: 3 statute miles and 500 ft below, 1,000 ft above, 2,000 ft horizontal from clouds in Classes C, D and E below 10,000 ft; Class B is 3 miles clear of clouds; Class G by day below 1,200 ft AGL is 1 mile clear of clouds; at or above 10,000 ft MSL it is 5 miles and 1,000 below, 1,000 above, 1 mile horizontal. Plus: no VFR beneath a ceiling below 1,000 ft in a surface area, and 3 miles ground visibility to take off, land or enter the pattern there.
- [[91.159|§ 91.159 — VFR cruising altitude or flight level]] — VFR cruising altitudes apply in level flight more than 3,000 ft above the surface: on a magnetic course of 0° through 179° fly an odd thousand-foot MSL altitude plus 500 ft; on 180° through 359° fly an even thousand plus 500 ft.
- [[91.215|§ 91.215 — ATC transponder and altitude reporting equipment and use]] — A Mode C transponder is required in Class A, B and C airspace, within the 30 NM Mode C veil of Class B primary airports from the surface to 10,000 ft, above Class B and C to 10,000 ft, and at and above 10,000 ft MSL except within 2,500 ft of the surface; when installed and operable it must be on and replying in all controlled airspace. ADS-B Out (§ 91.225) covers the same footprint.

### checkride

- [[61.56|§ 61.56 — Flight review]] — A flight review every 24 calendar months: at least 1 hour of flight training and 1 hour of ground training with an authorized instructor, covering the Part 91 rules and whatever maneuvers the instructor decides, ending in a logbook endorsement. A practical test or proficiency check in that window counts instead.
- [[61.57|§ 61.57 — Recent flight experience: Pilot in command]] — Recency to carry passengers: three takeoffs and three landings in the preceding 90 days as sole manipulator in the same category, class and type; at night (1 hour after sunset to 1 hour before sunrise) they must be to a full stop, made in that window.
- [[61.113|§ 61.113 — Private pilot privileges and limitations: Pilot in command]] — What a private pilot may not do: act as PIC for compensation or hire, or carry passengers or property for hire — with listed exceptions: flights incidental to a business, sharing pro rata expenses with passengers, charity events under § 91.146, search and location, and flying under BasicMed within the paragraph (i) limits.
- [[91.157|§ 91.157 — Special VFR weather minimums]] — Special VFR lets you operate in a surface area below basic VFR minimums with an ATC clearance, clear of clouds, with at least 1 statute mile flight visibility (and 1 mile ground visibility to take off or land); between sunrise and sunset unless you are instrument rated and the aircraft is equipped for IFR.
- [[91.207|§ 91.207 — Emergency locator transmitters]] — Most U.S. airplanes must carry an operable automatic ELT; batteries are replaced after more than 1 cumulative hour of use or at 50 percent of their useful life, the ELT is inspected within 12 calendar months, and exemptions include training flights within 50 NM of the departure airport and up to 90 days with the ELT removed for repair.
- [[91.211|§ 91.211 — Supplemental oxygen]] — Supplemental oxygen by cabin pressure altitude: the required crew must use it above 12,500 ft MSL for any part of the flight over 30 minutes, at all times above 14,000 ft, and every occupant must be provided with it above 15,000 ft.
- [[91.213|§ 91.213 — Inoperative instruments and equipment]] — Flying with inoperative equipment: without an MEL, a small non-turbine airplane may fly if the item is not required by the day-VFR type certification list, the aircraft's equipment list or KOEL, § 91.205 or any other rule for that kind of flight, or an AD; it is removed, or deactivated and placarded “Inoperative”, with maintenance recorded; and a pilot or mechanic determines it is no hazard.
- [[91.409|§ 91.409 — Inspections]] — An annual inspection within the preceding 12 calendar months is required before any operation; an aircraft carrying persons for hire, or used to give flight instruction for hire, also needs a 100-hour inspection, which may be exceeded by up to 10 hours to reach a place where it can be done.
