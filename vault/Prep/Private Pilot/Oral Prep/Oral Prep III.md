**Scenario 1.** Make the initial call to a Class D tower 10 miles out with the ATIS.
**Answer (study aid):** 'Springfield Tower, Cessna Three One Six Zero Foxtrot, one zero miles south at three thousand, inbound for landing with information Bravo.' Facility, full call sign, position, altitude, request, ATIS letter.
**Cites:** [[4-2-3|AIM 4-2-3 — Contact Procedures]], [[4-1-13|AIM 4-1-13 — Automatic Terminal Information Service (ATIS)]], [[91.129|§ 91.129 — Operations in Class D airspace]]

**Scenario 2.** You lose your radio in the pattern at a towered airport. Show me the light signals you expect.
**Answer (study aid):** Squawk 7600, keep the pattern, watch the tower: steady green is cleared to land, flashing green return for landing, steady red give way and keep circling, flashing red airport unsafe do not land, alternating red and green extreme caution. Acknowledge by rocking the wings by day or blinking the landing light at night.
**Find it:** § 91.125 is the rule; AIM 4-3-13 the table; 4-2-13 the arrival procedure.
**Cites:** [[91.125|§ 91.125 — ATC light signals]], [[4-3-13|AIM 4-3-13 — Traffic Control Light Signals]], [[4-2-13|AIM 4-2-13 — Communications with Tower when Aircraft Transmitter or Receiver or Both are Inoperative]], [[6-4-2|AIM 6-4-2 — Transponder Operation During Two‐way Communications Failure]]

**Scenario 3.** What transponder codes should you never dial through and why?
**Answer (study aid):** 7500 (hijack), 7600 (lost communications) and 7700 (emergency); passing through them momentarily can trigger alarms, so change codes carefully. VFR is 1200 and the transponder stays on with altitude reporting from pushback to parking.
**Cites:** [[4-1-20|AIM 4-1-20 — Transponder and ADS-B Out Operation]], [[91.215|§ 91.215 — ATC transponder and altitude reporting equipment and use]]

**Scenario 4.** You had a gear collapse on landing with no injuries. What are your reporting duties?
**Answer (study aid):** If it is an accident (substantial damage), notify the nearest NTSB office immediately and file the report within 10 days; listed incidents need immediate notification but a written report only if requested. Check NTSB Part 830 definitions.
**Cites:** [[7-7-2|AIM 7-7-2 — Aircraft Accident and Incident Reporting]]

**Scenario 5.** When would you declare an emergency, and what words do you use?
**Answer (study aid):** As soon as I am doubtful about fuel, position, weather or the airplane: that is an urgency condition, PAN-PAN; immediate danger is MAYDAY. Then the message: who I am calling, call sign and type, the problem, weather, intentions, position and heading, altitude, fuel in minutes, souls on board.
**Cites:** [[6-1-2|AIM 6-1-2 — Emergency Condition- Request Assistance Immediately]], [[6-3-1|AIM 6-3-1 — Distress and Urgency Communications]], [[6-3-2|AIM 6-3-2 — Obtaining Emergency Assistance]]

**Scenario 1.** You arrive at a non-towered airport with a wind sock showing a light wind favoring runway 18 while someone is using 36. What do you do?
**Answer (study aid):** Listen on the CTAF from 10 miles, announce, and fit into the flow in use if it is safe; the traffic already in the pattern has the flow. A light wind does not justify an opposing-direction conflict; wait or conform.
**Cites:** [[4-1-9|AIM 4-1-9 — Traffic Advisory Practices at Airports Without Operating Control Towers]], [[4-3-4|AIM 4-3-4 — Visual Indicators at Airports Without an Operating Control Tower]], [[91.113|§ 91.113 — Right-of-way rules: Except water operations]]

**Scenario 2.** Which way do you turn in the pattern, and how do you know if it is right traffic?
**Answer (study aid):** Left unless the airport shows otherwise: the segmented circle's traffic pattern indicators, the Chart Supplement, or the sectional's RP notation. § 91.126 and § 91.127 make left turns the rule.
**Cites:** [[91.126|§ 91.126 — Operating on or in the vicinity of an airport in Class G airspace]], [[91.127|§ 91.127 — Operating on or in the vicinity of an airport in Class E airspace]], [[4-3-4|AIM 4-3-4 — Visual Indicators at Airports Without an Operating Control Tower]]

**Scenario 3.** Two aircraft are on final to the same runway, one lower. Who has the right-of-way?
**Answer (study aid):** The lower aircraft, but it may not use that to cut in front of or overtake an aircraft already on final; an aircraft on final or landing has the right-of-way over others in flight or on the surface.
**Cites:** [[91.113|§ 91.113 — Right-of-way rules: Except water operations]], [[4-3-4|AIM 4-3-4 — Visual Indicators at Airports Without an Operating Control Tower]]

**Scenario 4.** The tower asks you to extend downwind for a jet on a 5-mile final. What are the hazards and what do you say?
**Answer (study aid):** Wake turbulence and losing sight of the field: stay at or above the jet's approach path and land beyond its touchdown point, keep the traffic in sight, and read back 'extend downwind, traffic in sight' or 'negative contact'. If the extension takes me into weather or terrain, say unable.
**Cites:** [[7-4-6|AIM 7-4-6 — Vortex Avoidance Procedures]], [[7-4-8|AIM 7-4-8 — Pilot Responsibility]], [[4-3-5|AIM 4-3-5 — Unexpected Maneuvers in the Airport Traffic Pattern]]

**Scenario 5.** What is the AWOS telling you when it says 'clear below one two thousand'?
**Answer (study aid):** No clouds detected within the ceilometer's 12,000 ft range; there could be clouds above. The one-minute weather is receivable within about 25 nm below 10,000 ft on the published frequency.
**Cites:** [[7-1-10|AIM 7-1-10 — Weather Observing Programs]], [[4-3-27|AIM 4-3-27 — Operations at Uncontrolled Airports With Automated Surface Observing System (ASOS)/Automated Weather Observing System (AWOS)]]
