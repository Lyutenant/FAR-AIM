**Scenario 1.** In slow flight the stall horn sounds continuously in a gust. What does the horn actually tell you?
**Answer (study aid):** That the wing is within a few knots or degrees of the critical angle of attack, not that a stall has occurred; recovery is always to reduce the angle of attack, and turbulence reduces the margin. The horn's range is set by the manufacturer.
**Cites:** [[91.9|§ 91.9 — Civil aircraft flight manual, marking, and placard requirements]]

**Scenario 1.** You are turning base to final, overshoot, and add rudder to tighten the turn. Why is that dangerous?
**Answer (study aid):** A cross-controlled skidding turn at low speed can produce a stall with the inside wing dropping into an incipient spin at an altitude with no recovery room; fix an overshoot with a go-around or a coordinated turn, never with rudder alone.
**Cites:** [[4-3-5|AIM 4-3-5 — Unexpected Maneuvers in the Airport Traffic Pattern]]

**Scenario 2.** What is the first action in any stall recovery?
**Answer (study aid):** Reduce the angle of attack: pitch down until the stall breaks, then add power, level the wings with coordinated controls and recover to a climb; power alone will not recover a stalled wing.
**Cites:** [[91.9|§ 91.9 — Civil aircraft flight manual, marking, and placard requirements]]

**Scenario 1.** During a power-on stall the airplane rolls left at the break. Why, and what do you do?
**Answer (study aid):** Left-turning tendencies (P-factor, torque, slipstream) at high power and angle of attack; keep the ball centered with right rudder, and at the break lower the nose first, then level the wings with rudder and coordinated aileron.
**Cites:** [[91.9|§ 91.9 — Civil aircraft flight manual, marking, and placard requirements]]

**Scenario 1.** What causes a spin and what is the generic recovery?
**Answer (study aid):** A stall with yaw. Recovery per the POH, generically PARE: power idle, ailerons neutral, rudder full opposite the rotation, elevator forward to break the stall, then neutral rudder and recover from the dive. Most training airplanes are approved for spins only in the utility category with specific loading.
**Cites:** [[91.9|§ 91.9 — Civil aircraft flight manual, marking, and placard requirements]], [[91.307|§ 91.307 — Parachutes and parachuting]]
