**Scenario 1.** The tower reports a wind shear alert with a 20-knot loss on departure. Do you go?
**Answer (study aid):** Not right now. A microburst or shear on climb-out can exceed the airplane's climb capability; wait a few minutes, get an update, and if departing add speed and use maximum performance.
**Cites:** [[4-3-7|AIM 4-3-7 — Low Level Wind Shear/Microburst Detection Systems]], [[7-1-24|AIM 7-1-24 — Microbursts]]

**Scenario 2.** Density altitude is 8,000 ft at a 4,000 ft runway. What changes in your takeoff?
**Answer (study aid):** Longer roll and a shallow climb: lean for best power per the POH, use the full runway, fix an abort point (a common check is 70 percent of rotation speed by half the runway), and use Vx over obstacles.
**Cites:** [[7-6-7|AIM 7-6-7 — Mountain Flying]]

**Scenario 3.** You are cleared for takeoff and see an aircraft on short final to the crossing runway. What do you do?
**Answer (study aid):** Hold, tell the tower, and wait for a new clearance; a clearance never relieves me of seeing and avoiding, and § 91.113 gives a landing aircraft the right-of-way.
**Cites:** [[91.113|§ 91.113 — Right-of-way rules: Except water operations]], [[4-3-18|AIM 4-3-18 — Taxiing]], [[4-4-15|AIM 4-4-15 — Use of Visual Clearing Procedures and Scanning Techniques]]

**Scenario 1.** Tower says 'cleared to land runway 27, hold short of runway 18'. Do you accept?
**Answer (study aid):** As a student or a pilot unfamiliar with LAHSO, no: 'unable, request full length'. Otherwise only if the published available landing distance exceeds my landing distance with margin, and I must read back the hold short.
**Cites:** [[4-3-11|AIM 4-3-11 — Pilot Responsibilities When Conducting Land and Hold Short Operations (LAHSO)]]

**Scenario 2.** You are high and fast on final at 300 ft. What defines a stabilized approach and when do you go around?
**Answer (study aid):** Constant angle, on speed, configured and trimmed with only small corrections needed by a set gate (say 300 to 500 ft); if I am not there, or a landing cannot be made in the first third of the runway, I go around immediately: full power, pitch for climb, flaps up in stages, and tell the tower.
**Cites:** [[4-3-5|AIM 4-3-5 — Unexpected Maneuvers in the Airport Traffic Pattern]], [[4-3-2|AIM 4-3-2 — Airports with an Operating Control Tower]]

**Scenario 3.** A heavy just landed on the parallel runway 2,000 ft away in a light crosswind blowing toward you. Concern?
**Answer (study aid):** Yes. The AIM warns of vortex drift onto a parallel runway closer than 2,500 ft; stay at or above the heavy's approach path and land beyond its touchdown point, or delay.
**Cites:** [[7-4-6|AIM 7-4-6 — Vortex Avoidance Procedures]], [[7-4-5|AIM 7-4-5 — Operations Problem Areas]]

**Scenario 1.** Why do you keep the nosewheel light on a soft field and how does ground effect help?
**Answer (study aid):** To keep it out of the soft surface and reduce drag and the chance of nosing over; lifting off early into ground effect lets the airplane accelerate to climb speed on reduced induced drag before leaving it.
**Cites:** [[91.13|§ 91.13 — Careless or reckless operation]]

**Scenario 1.** What is different about a soft-field landing and what wind consideration matters most?
**Answer (study aid):** Touch down as slowly as possible with the nose high and power on to cushion, keep the nosewheel off, and avoid braking; a gusty crosswind makes the slow, nose-high touchdown harder to control, so know your demonstrated crosswind and the go-around plan.
**Cites:** [[4-3-4|AIM 4-3-4 — Visual Indicators at Airports Without an Operating Control Tower]]

**Scenario 1.** On a short-field takeoff with a 50 ft obstacle, which speed do you climb at and why?
**Answer (study aid):** Vx, best angle of climb, until clear of the obstacle, then Vy; Vx gives the most altitude per distance. The POH obstacle-clearance chart assumes that technique and the chart's conditions.
**Cites:** [[91.9|§ 91.9 — Civil aircraft flight manual, marking, and placard requirements]]

**Scenario 2.** Your accelerate-stop distance exceeds the runway. What does that mean for the takeoff?
**Answer (study aid):** That if the engine fails at rotation you cannot stop on the pavement; brief an abort point well before it and consider not going.
**Cites:** [[4-3-6|AIM 4-3-6 — Use of Runways/Declared Distances]]

**Scenario 1.** When would you use a forward slip and what is the fuel and pitot concern?
**Answer (study aid):** To lose altitude without gaining speed on a high approach or without flaps; a sustained slip can unport a fuel tank pickup and some POHs prohibit slips with full flaps or warn of tail buffet and airspeed errors, so I check the POH limitations first.
**Cites:** [[91.9|§ 91.9 — Civil aircraft flight manual, marking, and placard requirements]]

**Scenario 1.** A deer runs onto the runway at 50 ft. Talk me through the go-around.
**Answer (study aid):** Full power immediately, carburetor heat off, pitch for a positive climb, flaps up to the go-around setting then in stages once climbing, offset to the side of the runway to keep the traffic in sight, trim, then talk to the tower or announce on the CTAF.
**Cites:** [[4-3-5|AIM 4-3-5 — Unexpected Maneuvers in the Airport Traffic Pattern]]

**Scenario 2.** You go around at a towered airport without a clearance to. Is that a violation?
**Answer (study aid):** No; the go-around is the PIC's decision and the safe action, and § 91.3 supports it. Tell the tower as soon as you are climbing and expect a new pattern instruction.
**Cites:** [[91.3|§ 91.3 — Responsibility and authority of the pilot in command]], [[91.123|§ 91.123 — Compliance with ATC clearances and instructions]]
