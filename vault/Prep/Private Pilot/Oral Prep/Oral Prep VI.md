**Scenario 1.** Show me how you chose your checkpoints and cruising altitude for today's cross-country.
**Answer (study aid):** Prominent, unique features 10 to 15 miles apart; altitude by the hemispheric rule, above terrain and obstacles with glide distance in mind, below the airspace I want to avoid, with winds aloft in mind; and a time and fuel plan per leg.
**Cites:** [[91.159|§ 91.159 — VFR cruising altitude or flight level]], [[91.119|§ 91.119 — Minimum safe altitudes: General]], [[3-1-5|AIM 3-1-5 — VFR Cruising Altitudes and Flight Levels]]

**Scenario 2.** The wind aloft is 270 true at 20 knots. Your course is magnetic; what do you convert and which way?
**Answer (study aid):** Winds aloft are true, so compute the wind correction angle on the true course, then apply variation (east is least) to get the magnetic heading, then deviation for the compass heading.
**Cites:** [[7-1-5|AIM 7-1-5 — Preflight Briefing]]

**Scenario 3.** Why does the magnetic compass lead or lag in turns and what do you do about it?
**Answer (study aid):** Northerly turning error: it lags turning through north and leads through south, and accelerates north on easterly and westerly headings (ANDS: accelerate north, decelerate south). Use timed turns or the heading indicator and read the compass only in straight, level, unaccelerated flight.
**Cites:** [[8-1-5|AIM 8-1-5 — Illusions in Flight]]

**Scenario 1.** You tune a VOR and the needle centers but you hear nothing. Can you use it?
**Answer (study aid):** No. The only positive identification is the Morse or voice ident; no ident means the station may be out of service or under maintenance.
**Cites:** [[1-1-3|AIM 1-1-3 — VHF Omni-directional Range (VOR)]]

**Scenario 2.** What can your panel-mount VFR GPS not do that an IFR unit does?
**Answer (study aid):** It has no RAIM alerting, its database need not be current for VFR and its antenna may be poorly placed; so cross-check with pilotage and VORs and never rely on it alone.
**Cites:** [[1-1-17|AIM 1-1-17 — Global Positioning System (GPS)]]

**Scenario 3.** You request flight following. What does the controller give you and what stays yours?
**Answer (study aid):** Traffic advisories by clock position, distance and direction when workload permits, and safety alerts; see-and-avoid, airspace, terrain and weather stay mine. When done, I tell them and squawk 1200.
**Cites:** [[4-1-15|AIM 4-1-15 — Radar Traffic Information Service]], [[4-1-17|AIM 4-1-17 — Radar Assistance to VFR Aircraft]], [[4-1-18|AIM 4-1-18 — Terminal Radar Services for VFR Aircraft]]

**Scenario 4.** Your ADS-B Out fails 20 miles from a Class C airport you want to land at. Options?
**Answer (study aid):** Ask the ATC facility for a deviation to continue to the airport with the inoperative unit (allowed any time); an unequipped aircraft would have needed the request an hour ahead. Tell them 'negative ADS-B'.
**Cites:** [[91.225|§ 91.225 — Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast (ADS-B) Out equipment and use]], [[4-5-7|AIM 4-5-7 — Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast (ADS-B) Services]]

**Scenario 1.** Forty minutes into a cross-country the ceiling ahead drops below your comfort. Show me the diversion.
**Answer (study aid):** Pick the nearest suitable airport (runway length, fuel, weather, terrain), turn toward it now using the chart's compass rose or the VOR, estimate heading, distance, time and fuel with the plotter or thumb, tell ATC or Flight Service, and update the flight plan.
**Cites:** [[6-2-1|AIM 6-2-1 — Radar Service for VFR Aircraft in Difficulty]], [[5-1-12|AIM 5-1-12 — Change in Flight Plan]], [[4-1-3|AIM 4-1-3 — Flight Service Stations]]

**Scenario 2.** You are given a heading by ATC that would take you into a cloud. What do you say?
**Answer (study aid):** 'Unable, VFR' with the reason; a vector to a VFR aircraft is advisory and never authorizes violating the cloud clearance minimums. Then request an alternative.
**Cites:** [[4-1-17|AIM 4-1-17 — Radar Assistance to VFR Aircraft]], [[91.123|§ 91.123 — Compliance with ATC clearances and instructions]]

**Scenario 1.** You are lost, fuel is at one hour, and the terrain is rising. What do you do first?
**Answer (study aid):** Climb (better radio and radar range), confirm the heading and time since the last fix, and immediately ask for help: 121.5 or the nearest approach control, squawk 7700, say lost, give last known position, altitude, fuel in minutes; VFR radar assistance and DF steers exist for this.
**Cites:** [[6-2-1|AIM 6-2-1 — Radar Service for VFR Aircraft in Difficulty]], [[6-3-2|AIM 6-3-2 — Obtaining Emergency Assistance]], [[4-1-3|AIM 4-1-3 — Flight Service Stations]]

**Scenario 2.** Is being lost an emergency?
**Answer (study aid):** The AIM says you are in at least an urgency condition the moment you become doubtful of your position; declaring early is what the FAA asks for, and delay costs lives.
**Cites:** [[6-1-2|AIM 6-1-2 — Emergency Condition- Request Assistance Immediately]]
