**Scenario 1.** Walk me through how you decided you were fit to fly this morning.
**Answer (study aid):** IMSAFE: no illness, no medication, manageable stress, no alcohol within 8 hours or lingering effects, rested, no emotional upset. The AIM gives it as the personal checklist.
**Cites:** [[8-1-1|AIM 8-1-1 — Fitness For Flight]], [[91.17|§ 91.17 — Alcohol or drugs]], [[61.53|§ 61.53 — Prohibition on operations during medical deficiency]]

**Scenario 2.** During preflight you find a bird's nest in the cowling and a loose fuel cap. Whose call is it?
**Answer (study aid):** Mine: § 91.7 makes the PIC responsible for determining the aircraft is in condition for safe flight. Remove the nest and check for damage, secure the cap, and if anything is unairworthy the flight does not go.
**Cites:** [[91.7|§ 91.7 — Civil aircraft airworthiness]]

**Scenario 3.** What NOTAMs did you check and where would a TFR appear?
**Answer (study aid):** Airport NOTAM (D)s for the departure, destination and alternates (runways, lighting, obstructions), FDC NOTAMs for TFRs and procedure changes, the ARTCC NOTAMs for GPS and airspace, and the Domestic Notices for temporary SUA; TFRs are FDC NOTAMs.
**Cites:** [[5-1-3|AIM 5-1-3 — Notice to Airmen (NOTAM) System]], [[91.103|§ 91.103 — Preflight action]]

**Scenario 4.** Someone you do not know asks a lot of questions about your airplane and how to start it. What do you do?
**Answer (study aid):** Treat it as a security concern: do not leave the airplane unattended and unlocked, report suspicious activity to the airport operator or the AOPA Airport Watch line, and follow the airport's security procedures.
**Cites:** [[8-1-8|AIM 8-1-8 — Judgment Aspects of Collision Avoidance]]

**Scenario 1.** What must you brief your passengers before takeoff, and when must belts be on?
**Answer (study aid):** How to fasten and unfasten the belt and shoulder harness, and to fasten them; belts and harnesses must be fastened for taxi, takeoff and landing, and everyone occupies an approved seat. A child under two may be held. Add the exits, the sterile cockpit expectation and what to do in an emergency.
**Cites:** [[91.107|§ 91.107 — Use of safety belts, shoulder harnesses, and child restraint systems]]

**Scenario 2.** Your tablet's chart database is two months old. Legal for VFR?
**Answer (study aid):** Legal, since no rule requires current data for VFR navigation, but the AIM says to use only current charts (revised every 56 days) and not to rely on an outdated database near critical airspace. Update it.
**Cites:** [[1-1-17|AIM 1-1-17 — Global Positioning System (GPS)]], [[5-1-1|AIM 5-1-1 — Preflight Preparation]], [[9-1-2|AIM 9-1-2 — Obtaining Aeronautical Charts]]

**Scenario 3.** May your passenger use a phone or tablet in the airplane?
**Answer (study aid):** On a VFR Part 91 flight, § 91.21 places no restriction; under IFR the PIC must determine it does not interfere with navigation or communication equipment.
**Cites:** [[91.21|§ 91.21 — Portable electronic devices]]

**Scenario 1.** The engine will not start after several attempts and smells of fuel. What do you suspect and do?
**Answer (study aid):** A flooded engine: follow the POH flooded-start procedure (typically mixture cut-off, throttle open, crank), watch for an induction fire (keep cranking to draw it in), and respect the starter duty cycle to avoid overheating it.
**Cites:** [[91.13|§ 91.13 — Careless or reckless operation]]

**Scenario 2.** Before you turn the key, what are you looking at outside?
**Answer (study aid):** Propeller area clear of people, chocks and towbar removed, brakes held, and a 'clear prop' call with a pause; the beacon on before start signals intent. Starting with someone near the propeller is careless operation.
**Cites:** [[4-3-24|AIM 4-3-24 — Use of Aircraft Lights]], [[91.13|§ 91.13 — Careless or reckless operation]]

**Scenario 1.** Ground says 'Runway 27, taxi via Alpha, hold short of Runway 18'. Read it back and tell me what it allows.
**Answer (study aid):** 'Runway 27 via Alpha, hold short of Runway 18.' It allows taxi along Alpha up to the runway 18 hold line and no further; crossing 18 needs an explicit clearance, and reaching 27 does not allow entering it either.
**Find it:** AIM 4-3-18 for taxi clearances and readbacks; § 91.129 for the Class D rules.
**Cites:** [[4-3-18|AIM 4-3-18 — Taxiing]], [[91.129|§ 91.129 — Operations in Class D airspace]]

**Scenario 2.** You see two solid and two dashed yellow lines across the taxiway with a red sign reading 9-27. Where do you stop?
**Answer (study aid):** On the solid-line side, with no part of the airplane past the marking, until cleared to cross or enter; the dashed side faces the runway.
**Cites:** [[2-3-5|AIM 2-3-5 — Holding Position Markings]], [[2-3-8|AIM 2-3-8 — Mandatory Instruction Signs]]

**Scenario 3.** After landing, when may you switch to ground and where is 'clear of the runway'?
**Answer (study aid):** When the tower tells you to contact ground; you are clear only when the whole airplane is past the runway holding position markings on the taxiway. Do not stop or turn around on the runway without approval.
**Cites:** [[4-3-21|AIM 4-3-21 — Exiting the Runway After Landing]], [[2-3-9|AIM 2-3-9 — Location Signs]]

**Scenario 4.** You are unsure of the route at a large unfamiliar airport at night. What do you ask for?
**Answer (study aid):** Progressive taxi instructions, and I stop and set the brakes if I become disoriented; the AIM says to tell the controller immediately and never guess across a runway.
**Cites:** [[4-3-18|AIM 4-3-18 — Taxiing]], [[4-3-19|AIM 4-3-19 — Taxi During Low Visibility]]

**Scenario 5.** What do blue lights, green lights and flashing yellow lights on the ground tell you at night?
**Answer (study aid):** Blue: taxiway edges. Green: taxiway centerline. Flashing yellow pairs: runway guard lights at a runway holding position. A red stop bar is never crossed while lit.
**Cites:** [[2-1-10|AIM 2-1-10 — Taxiway Lights]], [[4-3-24|AIM 4-3-24 — Use of Aircraft Lights]]

**Scenario 6.** The red in-pavement lights at the hold line are on but the tower cleared you to cross. Now what?
**Answer (study aid):** Stop and hold: runway status lights mean the runway is unsafe, so tell the tower you are holding for the lights and get the clearance re-confirmed. Never cross illuminated red lights.
**Cites:** [[2-1-6|AIM 2-1-6 — Runway Status Light (RWSL) System]]

**Scenario 1.** A Boeing 737 just took off ahead of you. When and how do you depart?
**Answer (study aid):** Wait for the wake interval (ask for the full 2 minutes if the tower offers less), rotate before the point where the jet rotated, climb above its flight path and turn upwind away from its track.
**Cites:** [[7-4-6|AIM 7-4-6 — Vortex Avoidance Procedures]], [[7-4-8|AIM 7-4-8 — Pilot Responsibility]]

**Scenario 2.** What is your plan if the engine quits at 300 ft after takeoff, and when do you brief it?
**Answer (study aid):** Before every takeoff: below the turnback altitude I land ahead within about 30 degrees of the nose, pitch for best glide immediately, flaps as needed, fuel and mixture off if time allows, and I brief the abort point on the runway too.
**Cites:** [[91.3|§ 91.3 — Responsibility and authority of the pilot in command]], [[6-3-2|AIM 6-3-2 — Obtaining Emergency Assistance]]

**Scenario 3.** The tower changes your departure runway while you are doing the run-up. What could go wrong?
**Answer (study aid):** Expectation bias: taxiing to the runway you planned and briefed. Re-read the new runway back, re-brief the departure and the emergency plan for the new direction, and check the wind and the compass against the new heading before taking the runway.
**Cites:** [[4-3-18|AIM 4-3-18 — Taxiing]], [[4-3-6|AIM 4-3-6 — Use of Runways/Declared Distances]]
