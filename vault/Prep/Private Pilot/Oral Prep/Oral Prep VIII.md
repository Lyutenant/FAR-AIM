**Scenario 1.** You blunder into a cloud at night. What do you do?
**Answer (study aid):** Believe the instruments, keep the wings level with the attitude indicator, hold altitude and heading, make a standard-rate 180-degree turn back to VMC, and tell ATC; if not instrument rated, say so and declare so radar can help you out.
**Cites:** [[6-2-1|AIM 6-2-1 — Radar Service for VFR Aircraft in Difficulty]], [[8-1-5|AIM 8-1-5 — Illusions in Flight]]

**Scenario 1.** The attitude indicator shows a nose-low, steeply banked attitude and the airspeed is increasing. Recovery?
**Answer (study aid):** Reduce power, level the wings, then raise the nose to the horizon; for nose-high and slowing it is power up, lower the nose, then level the wings. The order avoids a spiral or a stall.
**Cites:** [[8-1-5|AIM 8-1-5 — Illusions in Flight]]

**Scenario 2.** Why do instructors make you look down and then turn your head during instrument training?
**Answer (study aid):** To trigger the Coriolis illusion: a head movement in a prolonged turn creates the sensation of rotation in another axis. Prevention is not making sudden head movements and trusting the instruments over the inner ear.
**Cites:** [[8-1-5|AIM 8-1-5 — Illusions in Flight]]

**Scenario 1.** Under the hood, ATC says 'say intentions' after your flight following was terminated. Whom do you call for weather en route?
**Answer (study aid):** Flight Service on 122.2 or the frequency on the chart, or FIS-B on the tablet as a supplement; ATC facilities can also relay pilot reports and hazardous weather advisories.
