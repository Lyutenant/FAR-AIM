**Scenario 1.** When would you make an emergency descent and what limits it?
**Answer (study aid):** Fire, smoke, structural or pressurization problems, or a medical emergency; descend at the POH's emergency descent configuration and speed, never above Va in turbulence or Vne, with a bank to keep the load positive and the area clear.
**Cites:** [[91.9|§ 91.9 — Civil aircraft flight manual, marking, and placard requirements]], [[91.3|§ 91.3 — Responsibility and authority of the pilot in command]]

**Scenario 1.** The engine quits at 3,000 ft AGL. Talk me through it.
**Answer (study aid):** Pitch for best glide immediately, pick a field into the wind within glide range, run the restart flow (fuel selector, mixture, carb heat, magnetos, primer), then declare: 121.5 or the current frequency, squawk 7700, position and souls, and secure the engine before landing. ELT arms on impact; activate it manually if able.
**Cites:** [[6-3-2|AIM 6-3-2 — Obtaining Emergency Assistance]], [[6-2-2|AIM 6-2-2 — Transponder Emergency Operation]], [[6-2-4|AIM 6-2-4 — Emergency Locator Transmitter (ELT)]], [[91.3|§ 91.3 — Responsibility and authority of the pilot in command]]

**Scenario 2.** What does your ELT do after a crash and why does its type matter?
**Answer (study aid):** An armed ELT activates on crash forces and transmits for at least 48 hours; a 406 MHz unit is detected by satellite within minutes with your registration, while a 121.5 unit is heard only by overflying aircraft or ATC since satellites stopped monitoring it in 2009.
**Cites:** [[6-2-4|AIM 6-2-4 — Emergency Locator Transmitter (ELT)]], [[91.207|§ 91.207 — Emergency locator transmitters]]

**Scenario 1.** You have carburetor ice at 2,500 ft on a humid 20 C day. How do you know and what do you do?
**Answer (study aid):** A gradual RPM drop with a fixed-pitch propeller and rough running; full carb heat, expect a further drop then a rise as the ice melts, leave it on, and consider that the conditions favor icing at high humidity between roughly -7 and 21 C.
**Cites:** [[7-1-19|AIM 7-1-19 — PIREPs Relating to Airframe Icing]]

**Scenario 2.** Smoke appears in the cabin. What do you do and what does the AIM say about descending?
**Answer (study aid):** Identify the source: electrical (master off, then reintroduce circuits) or engine fire (fuel off, mixture cut, cabin heat off, land); the AIM notes an emergency requiring immediate action justifies any deviation, and smoke or fire is a case for an emergency descent.
**Cites:** [[91.3|§ 91.3 — Responsibility and authority of the pilot in command]], [[6-1-1|AIM 6-1-1 — Pilot Responsibility and Authority]]

**Scenario 3.** The cabin door pops open on climb-out. Now what?
**Answer (study aid):** Fly the airplane: it is loud but usually changes little, do not try to close it in flight, return and land, and brief passengers not to grab at it.
**Cites:** [[91.3|§ 91.3 — Responsibility and authority of the pilot in command]]

**Scenario 1.** How and when do you test the ELT, and what do you do before shutdown?
**Answer (study aid):** Analog ELTs only in the first 5 minutes after the hour, three sweeps maximum, never in flight; 406 MHz units per the manual. Before engine shutdown listen on 121.5 to catch an inadvertent activation.
**Cites:** [[6-2-4|AIM 6-2-4 — Emergency Locator Transmitter (ELT)]], [[91.207|§ 91.207 — Emergency locator transmitters]]

**Scenario 2.** You are flying over mountains in winter. What survival gear and why the 48 to 72 hours?
**Answer (study aid):** Water, warm clothing, shelter, signaling (mirror, PLB), fire starter and a first-aid kit: the AIM says the life expectancy of an injured survivor drops sharply in the first 24 hours and search effort depends on a filed flight plan and an ELT.
**Cites:** [[6-2-6|AIM 6-2-6 — Search and Rescue]], [[7-6-7|AIM 7-6-7 — Mountain Flying]]
