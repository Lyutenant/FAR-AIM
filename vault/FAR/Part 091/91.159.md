# § 91.159 — VFR cruising altitude or flight level

> [!info] Source
> eCFR Title 14, issue 2026-09-03 — [view on eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2026-09-03/title-14/section-91.159)

> [!study] Private Pilot study aid — curated, not official text
> **Gist:** VFR cruising altitudes apply in level flight more than 3,000 ft above the surface: on a magnetic course of 0° through 179° fly an odd thousand-foot MSL altitude plus 500 ft; on 180° through 359° fly an even thousand plus 500 ft.
> **Why it matters:** Asked on every written; applied on every cross-country.
> **Numbers:** Applies above 3,000 ft AGL · East (0°–179°): odd thousands + 500 · West (180°–359°): even thousands + 500
> **Watch out:** Magnetic course, not heading — a wind-correction angle does not change your altitude.
> **Watch out:** Below 3,000 ft AGL the rule does not apply at all.
> **Mnemonic (training-community, not FAA):** East is odd.
> **ACS:** [[PA.I.D#^pa-i-d-k2|PA.I.D.K2]] · [[PA.VI.A#^pa-vi-a-k4b|PA.VI.A.K4b]] — **Stage:** solo-xc

## Official Text

Except while holding in a holding pattern of 2 minutes or less, or while turning, each person operating an aircraft under VFR in level cruising flight more than 3,000 feet above the surface shall maintain the appropriate altitude or flight level prescribed below, unless otherwise authorized by ATC:

- **(a)** When operating below 18,000 feet MSL and—

    - **(1)** On a magnetic course of zero degrees through 179 degrees, any odd thousand foot MSL altitude + 500 feet (such as 3,500, 5,500, or 7,500); or

    - **(2)** On a magnetic course of 180 degrees through 359 degrees, any even thousand foot MSL altitude + 500 feet (such as 4,500, 6,500, or 8,500).

- **(b)** When operating above 18,000 feet MSL, maintain the altitude or flight level assigned by ATC.
