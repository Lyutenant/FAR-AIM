# § 91.151 — Fuel requirements for flight in VFR conditions

> [!info] Source
> eCFR Title 14, issue 2026-09-15 — [view on eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2026-09-15/title-14/section-91.151)

> [!study] Private Pilot study aid — curated, not official text
> **Gist:** Day VFR: enough fuel to fly to the first point of intended landing and then for at least 30 minutes at normal cruise; at night, at least 45 minutes.
> **Why it matters:** Two numbers the written asks directly and the examiner applies to your flight plan.
> **Numbers:** Day: destination + 30 minutes · Night: destination + 45 minutes
> **Watch out:** These are the legal minimums to begin the flight, computed considering wind and forecast weather; personal reserves should be larger.
> **ACS:** [[PA.I.D#^pa-i-d-k3c|PA.I.D.K3c]] · [[PA.I.D#^pa-i-d-r6|PA.I.D.R6]] — **Stage:** solo-xc

## Official Text

- **(a)** No person may begin a flight in an airplane under VFR conditions unless (considering wind and forecast weather conditions) there is enough fuel to fly to the first point of intended landing and, assuming normal cruising speed—

    - **(1)** During the day, to fly after that for at least 30 minutes; or

    - **(2)** At night, to fly after that for at least 45 minutes.

- **(b)** No person may begin a flight in a rotorcraft under VFR conditions unless (considering wind and forecast weather conditions) there is enough fuel to fly to the first point of intended landing and, assuming normal cruising speed, to fly after that for at least 20 minutes.
