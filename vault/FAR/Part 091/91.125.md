# § 91.125 — ATC light signals

> [!info] Source
> eCFR Title 14, issue 2026-09-03 — [view on eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2026-09-03/title-14/section-91.125)

> [!study] Private Pilot study aid — curated, not official text
> **Gist:** The light-gun signals are the regulation, not just an AIM table. The section is a single sentence pointing to its table: steady green, flashing green, steady red, flashing red, flashing white, and alternating red and green each have a fixed meaning on the ground and in flight.
> **Why it matters:** The examiner will ask you the signals from memory, and a radio failure in the pattern is when you will need them.
> **Watch out:** The same color means different things on the ground and in the air: steady red on the ground is stop, in flight it is give way and continue circling.
> **Watch out:** Flashing white applies only on the ground (return to starting point); there is no in-flight meaning.
> **Mnemonic (training-community, not FAA):** Green means go, red means no, white means home, red-and-green means careful.
> **ACS:** [[PA.III.A#^pa-iii-a-k3|PA.III.A.K3]] — **Stage:** pre-solo

## Official Text

ATC light signals have the meaning shown in the following table:

| Color and type of signal | Meaning with respect to aircraft on the surface | Meaning with respect to aircraft in flight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steady green | Cleared for takeoff | Cleared to land. |
| Flashing green | Cleared to taxi | Return for landing (to be followed by steady green at proper time). |
| Steady red | Stop | Give way to other aircraft and continue circling. |
| Flashing red | Taxi clear of runway in use | Airport unsafe—do not land. |
| Flashing white | Return to starting point on airport | Not applicable. |
| Alternating red and green | Exercise extreme caution | Exercise extreme caution. |
