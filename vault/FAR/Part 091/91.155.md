# § 91.155 — Basic VFR weather minimums

> [!info] Source
> eCFR Title 14, issue 2026-09-15 — [view on eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2026-09-15/title-14/section-91.155)

> [!study] Private Pilot study aid — curated, not official text
> **Gist:** Basic VFR weather minimums by airspace: 3 statute miles and 500 ft below, 1,000 ft above, 2,000 ft horizontal from clouds in Classes C, D and E below 10,000 ft; Class B is 3 miles clear of clouds; Class G by day below 1,200 ft AGL is 1 mile clear of clouds; at or above 10,000 ft MSL it is 5 miles and 1,000 below, 1,000 above, 1 mile horizontal. Plus: no VFR beneath a ceiling below 1,000 ft in a surface area, and 3 miles ground visibility to take off, land or enter the pattern there.
> **Why it matters:** The table is the single most-tested piece of the FAR on the private written, and paragraphs (c) and (d) are the oral's follow-up.
> **Numbers:** 3 SM in Classes B, C, D and E below 10,000 ft · 500 below, 1,000 above, 2,000 horizontal · 5 SM at or above 10,000 ft MSL · At or above 10,000 ft MSL: 1,000 below, 1,000 above, 1 SM horizontal · Surface area: ceiling at least 1,000 ft · Surface area: 3 SM ground visibility to take off, land or enter the pattern
> **Watch out:** Class G at night below 1,200 ft AGL jumps to 3 miles and 500/1,000/2,000 — night Class G is treated like Class E.
> **Watch out:** “Clear of clouds” in Class B is not a distance: you may not enter them.
> **Mnemonic (training-community, not FAA):** 3-152: 3 miles, 500 below, 1,000 above, 2,000 horizontal — the everyday minimums.
> **ACS:** [[PA.I.E#^pa-i-e-k1|PA.I.E.K1]] · [[PA.VIII.A#^pa-viii-a-r1|PA.VIII.A.R1]] — **Stage:** solo-xc

- **(b)** *Class G Airspace.* Notwithstanding the provisions of paragraph (a) of this section, the following operations may be conducted in Class G airspace below 1,200 feet above the surface:

    - **(1)** *Helicopter.* A helicopter may be operated clear of clouds in an airport traffic pattern within 1/2 mile of the runway or helipad of intended landing if the flight visibility is not less than 1/2 statute mile.

    - **(2)** *Airplane, powered parachute, or weight-shift-control aircraft.* If the visibility is less than 3 statute miles but not less than 1 statute mile during night hours and you are operating in an airport traffic pattern within 1/2 mile of the runway, you may operate an airplane, powered parachute, or weight-shift-control aircraft clear of clouds.

- **(c)** Except as provided in § 91.157, no person may operate an aircraft beneath the ceiling under VFR within the lateral boundaries of controlled airspace designated to the surface for an airport when the ceiling is less than 1,000 feet.

- **(d)** Except as provided in § 91.157 of this part, no person may take off or land an aircraft, or enter the traffic pattern of an airport, under VFR, within the lateral boundaries of the surface areas of Class B, Class C, Class D, or Class E airspace designated for an airport—

    - **(1)** Unless ground visibility at that airport is at least 3 statute miles; or

    - **(2)** If ground visibility is not reported at that airport, unless flight visibility during landing or takeoff, or while operating in the traffic pattern is at least 3 statute miles.

- **(e)** For the purpose of this section, an aircraft operating at the base altitude of a Class E airspace area is considered to be within the airspace directly below that area.
