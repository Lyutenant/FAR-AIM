# § 91.7 — Civil aircraft airworthiness

> [!info] Source
> eCFR Title 14, issue 2026-09-15 — [view on eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2026-09-15/title-14/section-91.7)

> [!study] Private Pilot study aid — curated, not official text
> **Gist:** Two sentences that carry a lot of weight: no one may operate a civil aircraft unless it is airworthy, and the PIC is responsible for deciding whether it is in condition for safe flight and must discontinue the flight when an unairworthy condition occurs.
> **Why it matters:** The preflight inspection and every inoperative-equipment question come back to this rule: the decision is yours, not the mechanic's.
> **Numbers:** PIC decides whether the aircraft is safe for flight
> **Watch out:** Airworthy has two parts: conforms to its type design and is in condition for safe operation. A fresh annual does not settle the second part today.
> **ACS:** [[PA.I.B#^pa-i-b-k1e|PA.I.B.K1e]] · [[PA.II.A#^pa-ii-a-k3d|PA.II.A.K3d]] — **Stage:** pre-solo

## Official Text

- **(a)** No person may operate a civil aircraft unless it is in an airworthy condition.

- **(b)** The pilot in command of a civil aircraft is responsible for determining whether that aircraft is in condition for safe flight. The pilot in command shall discontinue the flight when unairworthy mechanical, electrical, or structural conditions occur.
