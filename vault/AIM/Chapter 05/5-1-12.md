# AIM 5-1-12 — Change in Flight Plan

> [!info] Source
> FAA Aeronautical Information Manual, Basic with Change 1, 2 and 3 (effective 2026-07-09) — [view on FAA](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap5_section_1.html#5-1-12)

> [!study] Private Pilot study aid — curated, not official text
> **Gist:** A change in flight plan includes altitude, route, destination and also speed: tell ATC when your true airspeed at cruise differs from the filed value by 5 percent or 10 knots, whichever is greater. Changes to a filed plan should be made with the original provider more than 46 minutes before departure; closer than that, through ATC or FSS.
> **Why it matters:** It is a knowledge-test number (5 percent or 10 knots) and the practical answer to 'I filed for 100 knots and am doing 115'.
> **Numbers:** Speed change of 5 percent or 10 knots · Changes more than 46 minutes before departure go to the original filer
> **ACS:** [[PA.I.D#^pa-i-d-k5|PA.I.D.K5]] — **Stage:** solo-xc

## Official Text

- **a.** In addition to altitude or flight level, destination and/or route changes, increasing or decreasing the speed of an aircraft constitutes a change in a flight plan. Therefore, at any time the average true airspeed at cruising altitude between reporting points varies or is expected to vary from that given in the flight plan by plus or minus 5 percent, or 10 knots, whichever is greater, ATC should be advised.

- **b.** All changes to existing flight plans should be completed more than 46 minutes prior to the proposed departure time. Changes must be made with the initial flight plan service provider. If the initial flight plan's service provider is unavailable, filers may contact an ATC facility or FSS to make the necessary revisions. Any revision 46 minutes or less from the proposed departure time must be coordinated through an ATC facility or FSS.
