# AIM 3-1-5 — VFR Cruising Altitudes and Flight Levels

> [!info] Source
> FAA Aeronautical Information Manual, Basic with Change 1, 2 and 3 (effective 2026-07-09) — [view on FAA](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap3_section_1.html#3-1-5)

> [!study] Private Pilot study aid — curated, not official text
> **Gist:** VFR cruising altitudes above 3,000 ft AGL and below 18,000 ft MSL: magnetic course 0 to 179 degrees, odd thousands plus 500; 180 to 359, even thousands plus 500. Above 18,000 the same pattern applies to flight levels, but that is Class A and IFR.
> **Why it matters:** The examiner will ask for a cruising altitude on your planned route; it must fit this table.
> **Numbers:** Applies above 3,000 ft AGL and below 18,000 ft MSL · 0 to 179: odd thousands plus 500 · 180 to 359: even thousands plus 500
> **Watch out:** It is magnetic course, not heading; a strong crosswind does not change your altitude.
> **Watch out:** Below 3,000 ft AGL the rule does not apply, but a sensible altitude still does.
> **Mnemonic (training-community, not FAA):** East is odd: 0 to 179 fly odd thousands plus 500 (NEodd SWeven).
> **ACS:** [[PA.I.D#^pa-i-d-k2|PA.I.D.K2]] · [[PA.VI.A#^pa-vi-a-k4b|PA.VI.A.K4b]] — **Stage:** solo-xc
